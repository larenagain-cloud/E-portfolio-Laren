About this portfolio

The purpose

This e-portfolio is a requirement for the subject Technology for Teaching and Learning. It collects the assessment output of each of the four modules, with a short definition and description of the topic, and a reflection structured on Driscoll's model.

The model asks three questions: What? (a description of the experience), So what? (an analysis of what it means) and Now what? (the actions that follow).

Module 01

Timeline of Educational Technology

Definition

Educational technology is the study and ethical practice of facilitating learning and improving performance by creating, using and managing appropriate tools, processes and resources.

Description

This module traces how the tools of teaching changed over time, and how each new tool reshaped the roles of teacher and learner.

  • EarlyOral instruction, the hornbook and the chalkboard.
  • 1900s–1950sPrint, film, radio and the first instructional television.
  • 1950s–1970sTeaching machines and programmed instruction.
  • 1980s–1990sPersonal computers, multimedia and the Internet.
  • 2000s–2010sLearning management systems, mobile devices and open online courses.
  • 2020sRemote and blended learning, and artificial intelligence in the classroom.
Assessment Output

Educational Technology Timeline

A chronological timeline of major milestones in educational technology, each paired with a short note on its effect on teaching practice.

View output
Reflection

Driscoll's model

1

What?

What did I do and learn?

I built a timeline of educational technology and found that each era's tools grew out of the needs and limits of the one before it.

2

So what?

Why does it matter?

As an engineering graduate I had thought of technology as hardware. The timeline showed me that a tool only improves learning when it is matched to a clear teaching purpose.

3

Now what?

What will I do next?

I will choose tools by the learning outcome they support, not by novelty, and I will keep up with new developments in the field.

Module 02

Information Disorders

Definition

Information disorder is the umbrella term for false or harmful content that circulates in society, whether or not it is shared with the intent to cause harm.

Description

The module separates three types of information disorder and the skills needed to resist them.

  • MisinformationFalse information shared without intent to harm.
  • DisinformationFalse information created or shared deliberately to deceive or harm.
  • MalinformationGenuine information shared to cause harm, such as leaked private details.

Media and information literacy, source verification and fact-checking are the main defenses, and teachers have a particular duty to model them.

Assessment Output

Information Disorder Analysis

An analysis that classifies sample posts as misinformation, disinformation or malinformation, with the verification steps used to check each claim.

View output
Reflection

Driscoll's model

1

What?

What did I do and learn?

I learned to tell the three types of information disorder apart and practiced checking the origin, evidence and date of online claims.

2

So what?

Why does it matter?

I realized I had shared content without checking it. Students are exposed to the same content daily, so verifying before sharing is a basic habit of a responsible digital citizen.

3

Now what?

What will I do next?

I will verify before I share, and I will teach learners simple checks they can apply to any source.

Module 03

Data Privacy Act of 2012

Definition

Republic Act No. 10173, the Data Privacy Act of 2012, is the Philippine law that protects the fundamental right to privacy of individuals while allowing the free flow of information needed for innovation and growth.

Description

The Act governs how personal information is collected, stored, used and disposed of, and created the National Privacy Commission to enforce it.

  • PrinciplesTransparency, legitimate purpose and proportionality.
  • Data subject rightsTo be informed, to access, to object, to correct, to erase or block, and to claim damages.
  • ObligationsOrganizations must secure personal data and report serious breaches.

In schools, it applies to student records, grades, photos and any learning platform that collects learner data.

Assessment Output

Data Privacy in the Classroom

A case-based output applying the Act's principles and data subject rights to common classroom situations involving learner data.

View output
Reflection

Driscoll's model

1

What?

What did I do and learn?

I studied the provisions of RA 10173 and applied them to realistic school scenarios, such as posting class lists or student photos online.

2

So what?

Why does it matter?

Teachers hold personal data about minors. Handling it carelessly is not just poor practice, it can be a legal violation that harms learners.

3

Now what?

What will I do next?

I will collect only the data I need, get consent where required, store it securely and be open with learners about how it is used.

Module 04

Development of Technology

Definition

Technology development is the continuing process of inventing, improving and applying tools, systems and methods to solve problems and extend human capability.

Description

This module looks at how technology advances and how those advances change teaching and learning.

  • DriversHuman needs, scientific discovery, economic demand and earlier innovations.
  • ImpactWider access to information, flexible and personalized learning, new forms of collaboration.
  • ChallengesThe digital divide, distraction, over-reliance and the need for ongoing teacher training.
Assessment Output

Technology Development Report

A report on a chosen technology's development and its practical use in teaching, including its benefits and limits in the learning environment.

View output
Reflection

Driscoll's model

1

What?

What did I do and learn?

I examined how a technology developed over time and what changes it brought to how lessons are delivered and how students learn.

2

So what?

Why does it matter?

Technology moves faster than curricula. Without access and training, new tools widen the gap between learners instead of closing it.

3

Now what?

What will I do next?

I will keep learning new tools, test them with a clear purpose, and design lessons that still work for learners with limited access.
